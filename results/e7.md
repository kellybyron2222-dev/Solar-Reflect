# E7 — How many satellites for useful light

**Campaign.** After E6. One site, civil night. Count vehicles that meet **both** parked \(I^*\) and \(T^*\). Not a global constellation. No new class. No cost.

## 1. Question

At each grid height, how many co-aimed, sequential copies of a marker are required so a dark site gets useful brightness **and** useful duration?

## 2. Definition

Civil-night snapshot (E6). \(\eta=1\) unless stated.

\[
N_I=\frac{I^*}{I_\text{one}},\qquad
N_T=\frac{T^*}{T_\text{useful}},\qquad
N=N_I\,N_T
\]

- \(N_I\): how many of that marker must aim at the same patch to hit \(I^*\) (linear while \(N_I A\ll A_\text{image}\)).
- \(N_T\): how many replacements to cover \(T^*\) (shutter ratio; area does not enter).
- \(N\): vehicles for **one site, one night window**. Not Earth coverage.

C-moon \(T^*=\) one useful pass \(\Rightarrow N_T=1\). C-day \(T^*\) is n/a \(\Rightarrow\) quote \(N_I\) only. \(N<1\) means one vehicle already exceeds that test. If \(T_\text{useful}=0\), there is **no pass** — \(N\) is not a number.

**Does not:** stack past one resolved sun (C-day fill \(>1\) is bookkeeping). Does not schedule orbits. Does not include weather or umbra.

## 4. \(N=N_I N_T\) at civil night, \(\eta=1\)

Four significant figures. 400 km omitted (no pass).

### C-moon — \(0.003\,\mathrm{W/m^2}\), 1 pass (\(N_T=1\))

| \(h\) | M18 \(N\) | M55 \(N\) | M1km \(N\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 625 km | 1.706 | 0.1827 | \(5.526\times 10^{-4}\) |
| 1000 km | 1.874 | 0.2007 | \(6.072\times 10^{-4}\) |
| 2000 km | 3.955 | 0.4236 | \(1.281\times 10^{-3}\) |

One M55 or M1km is enough. M18 needs **two** at 625–1000 km, **four** at 2000 km (ceil).

### C-light — \(0.1\,\mathrm{W/m^2}\), \(20\,\mathrm{min}\)

## 5. If each satellite is already large enough (\(N_I=1\))

Set \(A=A_\mathrm{req}(I^*)\). Then \(N=N_T\). This is the shutter floor: you cannot beat it by enlarging the foil.

| Class | 625 km | 1000 km | 2000 km | \(A_\mathrm{req}\) @ 625 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-moon | 1 | 1 | 1 | \(553\,\mathrm{m}^2\) (~24 m square) |
| C-light | 7.120 | 3.474 | 1.591 | \(1.84\times 10^4\,\mathrm{m}^2\) (~136 m) |
| C-weak | 7.120 | 3.474 | 1.591 | \(9.21\times 10^6\,\mathrm{m}^2\) (~3.0 km) |
| C-energy | 64.08 | 31.27 | 14.32 | \(3.68\times 10^7\,\mathrm{m}^2\) (~6.1 km) |
| C-day | n/a | n/a | n/a | \(1.84\times 10^8\,\mathrm{m}^2\) (fill 1.93) |

## 6. Realistic \(\eta=0.675\)

\(N_T\) unchanged. \(N_I\) and \(N\) scale by \(1/0.675=1.481\). C-light M18 @ 625 km: \(N=600\). C-weak M1km @ 625 km: \(N=97.1\).

## 7. Check cited

- \(N_I\times I_\text{one}=I^*\) (E6 invert).
- \(N_T\times T_\text{useful}=T^*\) (E6 §5).
- Depression 0 still matches the nadir kernel (E6 tests).
- C-day \(N_I\) is the same linear invert as E6 §6; not stacked suns.

## 8. Observation

Useful light is two multipliers, not one fleet number. Brightness is copies on the same patch; duration is a train. An 18 m foil can do moonlight at 625 km with **two** craft and one pass, or street-class watts-and-minutes with **~400**. Weak PV is tens of 1 km foils in a 20 min train, or seven already-huge sheets. Three-hour energy is thousands of 1 km foils if you pretend they stack, or ~64 full-size sheets in a train. 400 km is out: the night site never clears the shutter.

**Not:** Earth coverage. Not 50,000 as a physics result. Not a cost. Not midnight.
