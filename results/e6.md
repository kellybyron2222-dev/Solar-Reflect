# E6 — Night-value snapshot

**Campaign.** Framed amendment: score the case where a foil can create value (ground already dark), not terminator-nadir twilight. Not a replacement of `irradiance()`. Same filled-image law (Canady eq. 9). No new \(I^*\).

## 1. Question

At closest approach, with the satellite still on the terminator (sunlit) and the site in **civil night** (Sun \(6^\circ\) below the horizon), what are \(I\), \(D\), and \(T\)?

Local midnight at nadir is **not** this state: that satellite is usually in umbra (\(I=0\)). LEO cannot see a midnight site from a sunlit foil except near the poles in season. Civil night is the first standard “night,” and at 625 km it is still inside the \(\varepsilon\ge 30^\circ\) shutter. Nautical night (\(-12^\circ\)) is not: \(\varepsilon_\max=18^\circ\), \(T_\text{useful}=0\).

## 8. Observation

The valuable case is dimmer and shorter than the dusk envelope, and it is **early night**, not 2 a.m. An 18 m foil at 625 km no longer clears moonlight-class \(I\). Street-class \(I\) fails for M55 even at \(\eta=1\). Hours still require a train. 400 km cannot hold civil night above \(30^\circ\).

**Not:** a midnight kernel. Not umbra along the pass. Not \(\tau(\varepsilon)\). Not a company score.
